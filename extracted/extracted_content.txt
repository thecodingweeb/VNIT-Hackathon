1. Executive Summary
Pench Tiger Reserve (PTR), spanning over 1,920 sq km across Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra, is one of India's
premier tiger habitats supporting an estimated 40-80+ individual tigers. The reserve deploys hundreds of camera traps
across its core (~668 sq km) and buffer (~1,252 sq km) zones, generating millions of images annually. Current manual
processing workflows are unsustainable: 70-95% of captured images are blanks triggered by vegetation, wind, or
thermal anomalies, and individual tiger identification requires trained experts to laboriously compare stripe patterns
across thousands of images.
This PRD defines the requirements for an Automated Camera Trap Triage and Individual Tiger Movement Intelligence
System - an end-to-end AI/ML pipeline that will:
>Automatically filter and quarantine blank images, recovering 70-95% of storage and analyst time.
>Identify individual tigers through unique stripe-pattern biometrics using deep learning.
>Build and maintain a persistent, queryable identification database linking individuals to spatiotemporal
capture data.
>Generate per-tiger area occupancy maps with home-range estimates, activity centroids, and territorial
overlap visualization.
>Detect behavioural deviations and raise evidence-backed alerts for range shifts, station intrusions,
buffer-zone approaches, and prolonged absences.
The system is designed to transform raw camera-trap data into actionable intelligence for forest department staff,
supporting proactive conservation management, anti-poaching operations, and human-wildlife conflict mitigation at
Pench Tiger Reserve.

2. Problem Statement & Background
2.1 Core Problem
Camera trapping is the gold standard for non-invasive tiger monitoring, but the sheer volume of data generated
overwhelms manual processing capacity. At Pench Tiger Reserve, the key challenges are:
>>Data Overload: Each camera trap station can capture 500-2,000 images per deployment cycle. With 200+
stations, a single survey generates 100,000-400,000 images, of which 70-95% are blank.
>>Manual Identification Bottleneck: Individual tiger identification by stripe-pattern matching is currently
performed by a small team of experts, taking weeks to months per
survey cycle.
>>Delayed Insights: By the time movement data is analyzed, the intelligence is outdated - range shifts,
territorial conflicts, or emergencies may have already escalated.
>>No Automated Alerting: There is no system to proactively flag anomalies such as tigers moving toward
village buffer zones, disappearing from regular patrol areas, or exhibiting
stress-indicative movement patterns.
2.2 Pench Tiger Reserve Context
>>Location: Straddling Seoni and Chhindwara districts in Madhya Pradesh and Nagpur district in
Maharashtra, along the southern slopes of the Satpura Range.
>>Total Protected Area: Approximately 1,920 sq km (MP side: core ~411 sq km, buffer ~768 sq km;
Maharashtra side: core ~257 sq km, buffer ~484 sq km).
>>Tiger Population: Estimated 40-80+ individual tigers based on the 2022 All-India Tiger Estimation (Phase
IV camera trapping and SECR modeling).
>>Monitoring Infrastructure: Systematic camera trapping grids deployed across Turia, Karmajhiri, Sillari,
and other management zones. Data feeds into NTCA's national monitoring
framework.
>>Key Challenges: Remote terrain with limited connectivity, seasonal accessibility constraints during
monsoons, overlap with human settlements in buffer zones, and rising human-wildlife
conflict incidents.
2.3 Current Workflow Limitations
>Images are manually downloaded from SD cards at each station during periodic field visits.
>Blank images are visually screened and deleted by field staff - a tedious, error-prone process.
>Tiger identification relies on side-by-side stripe comparison using printed photographs or basic image
viewers.
>Occupancy and movement analysis is performed retrospectively using spreadsheets and manual GIS
plotting.
>No real-time or near-real-time alerting mechanism exists for anomalous tiger behaviour.
>Data from MP and Maharashtra sides is often siloed, preventing holistic landscape-level analysis.

3. Project Objectives
3.1 Primary Objectives
>Automate the classification and quarantine of blank/empty camera trap images with >95% accuracy,
reducing manual screening effort by at least 80%.
>Develop a deep-learning-based individual tiger identification system using stripe-pattern biometrics,
capable of matching against a persistent and growing catalogue of known individuals.
>Build a persistent, queryable database that links each identified individual tiger to every image, camera
station, GPS location, and timestamp where it was captured.
>Generate per-tiger area occupancy maps including capture locations, home-range estimates (using
KDE/MCP), activity centroids, estimated occupied area, and inter-individual territorial overlap.
>Implement an automated deviation and trend alerting engine that compares each processing run against
historical baselines and raises evidence-backed alerts for anomalous tiger behaviour.
3.2 Secondary Objectives
>Reduce the end-to-end survey processing turnaround from weeks/months to hours/days.
>Provide forest department staff with intuitive, map-based dashboards requiring minimal technical training.
>Enable landscape-level analysis by integrating data from both the Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra sides
of Pench.
>Ensure compliance with NTCA data security protocols for sensitive tiger location data.
>Design the system for extensibility to other species (leopards, wild dogs, sloth bears) and other tiger
reserves.
>Support both cloud-based and on-premise deployment models to accommodate varying connectivity and
security requirements.

5. System Overview & Architecture
5.1 High-Level Architecture
The system follows a modular, pipeline-based architecture with four core processing modules and supporting
infrastructure layers. The design adopts a hybrid Edge-to-Cloud model optimized for the remote, low-connectivity
environment of a tiger reserve.
5.2 Architectural Layers
>>Data Ingestion Layer: Handles bulk upload of raw image directories from SD cards, network-connected
cameras, or edge devices. Supports batch and incremental ingestion with
deduplication.
>>AI/ML Processing Pipeline: Sequential processing through: (1) Blank Image Filtering, (2) Tiger Detection
& Cropping, (3) Stripe Pattern Extraction, (4) Individual Matching/Enrollment.
>>Persistent Data Store: Relational database (PostgreSQL with PostGIS extension) storing individual
profiles, image metadata, GPS coordinates, match confidence scores, and
human-review audit trails.
>>Spatial Analytics Engine: Computes per-tiger home ranges (KDE 95%/50% isopleths), activity centroids,
area estimates, and territorial overlap matrices. Regenerated on every
processing run.
>>Deviation Detection & Alerting: Statistical engine comparing current-run outputs against historical
baselines. Generates classified alerts with evidence and confidence
levels.
>>Visualization & Reporting Layer: Web-based dashboard with interactive maps, individual tiger profiles,
alert feeds, and exportable reports (PDF, CSV, Shapefile).
5.3 Deployment Model
>Primary deployment: On-premise server at the reserve headquarters with GPU capability (NVIDIA
RTX-class or better) for AI inference.
>Optional cloud tier: AWS/GCP for heavy batch processing, multi-reserve aggregation, and disaster
recovery.
>Edge processing (future): NVIDIA Jetson or Google Coral devices at high-priority stations for real-time
blank filtering and alert generation.
>All sensitive tiger location data remains within government-controlled infrastructure per NTCA protocols.

6. Functional Requirements
6.1 Module 1: Blank Image Filtering & Removal
Purpose: Automatically classify each camera trap frame as blank or containing a subject, quarantine blanks, and report
savings.
>>FR-1.1 Batch Ingestion: The system must accept raw image directories (JPEG, PNG, TIFF, BMP) and
recursively scan all subdirectories. It must handle directory structures organized
by station, date, or camera unit.
>>FR-1.2 AI-Based Classification: Each image must be processed through an object detection model
(MegaDetector V6 or equivalent YOLO/RT-DETR architecture) to detect
animals, people, and vehicles. Images with no detections above the
configurable confidence threshold (default: 0.2) are classified as blank.
>>FR-1.3 Confidence Thresholding: The confidence threshold for blank classification must be configurable
by administrators (range: 0.05 - 0.50). Images near the threshold
boundary (e.g., within 0.05 of threshold) must be flagged for optional
human review.
>>FR-1.4 Safe Quarantine: Blank images must NOT be permanently deleted. They must be moved to a
quarantine directory preserving the original folder structure. Quarantine must be
reversible - any image can be restored to the working dataset with one action.
>>FR-1.5 Processing Report: After each filtering run, the system must generate a summary report
containing: total images scanned, number classified as blank, number
retained, confidence distribution histogram, estimated storage saved (MB/GB),
estimated processing time saved, and per-station breakdown.
>>FR-1.6 Burst/Sequence Handling: For camera traps operating in burst mode (3-5 rapid frames per
trigger), the system must analyse the entire burst and retain the full
sequence if any single frame contains a subject.
>>FR-1.7 Performance Target: The blank classifier must achieve a minimum recall of 95% (i.e., miss no
more than 5% of true subjects) and a minimum precision of 90% (i.e., no
more than 10% of retained images are actually blank).

6.2 Module 2: Individual Tiger Identification via Stripe Pattern
Purpose: Detect tigers in retained images, extract unique stripe-pattern biometrics, match against a persistent
catalogue, and build an individual-linked database.
>>FR-2.1 Tiger Detection & Cropping: From each retained image, the system must detect tiger instances
using an object detection model (YOLO-based or Faster R-CNN).
Each detected tiger must be cropped with appropriate padding
(10-20% beyond bounding box) for downstream analysis.
>>FR-2.2 Flank Isolation: The system must identify the flank region (left or right side) of each detected tiger.
Flank orientation (L/R) must be recorded as metadata, as stripe patterns differ
between sides.
>>FR-2.3 Stripe Pattern Extraction: The system must extract discriminative stripe-pattern features from the
isolated flank using deep feature extraction (CNN backbone such as
InceptionResNetV2, DenseNet, or a fine-tuned ResNet-50). Features
must be stored as embedding vectors in the database.
>>FR-2.4 Catalogue Matching: Each extracted stripe embedding must be compared against all existing
individuals in the catalogue using a similarity metric (cosine similarity or
learned distance via Siamese/Triplet network). The system must return a
ranked list of candidate matches with confidence scores.
>>FR-2.5 Automatic vs. Ambiguous Matching: Matches exceeding a high-confidence threshold (e.g.,
cosine similarity > 0.85) must be applied automatically.
Matches falling in the ambiguous zone (e.g., 0.60 - 0.85)
must be surfaced to a human reviewer via a review queue
with side-by-side comparison UI. Matches below the low
threshold (< 0.60) must trigger new individual enrollment.
>>FR-2.6 New Individual Enrollment: When no confident match is found, the system must create a new
individual record with an auto-generated unique identifier (e.g.,
PTR-T-001), store the reference embedding, and associate all capture
metadata.
>>FR-2.7 Persistent Database: The system must maintain a persistent, queryable database (PostgreSQL
with PostGIS) linking each identified individual to: all associated images (with
file paths), camera station ID, GPS coordinates (latitude/longitude), capture
timestamp, flank orientation, match confidence score, and review status
(auto-matched / human-verified / pending-review).
>>FR-2.8 Catalogue Growth & Re-identification: The catalogue must grow incrementally across processing
runs. Previously identified individuals must be re-identified
in new captures. The system must handle at least 200
individual tiger profiles with 10,000+ associated images
without performance degradation.
>>FR-2.9 Human Review Interface: Ambiguous matches must be presented in a web-based review interface
showing: the query image, top-K candidate matches from the catalogue,
side-by-side flank comparison, confidence scores, and
accept/reject/merge actions for the reviewer.

6.3 Module 3: Tiger-wise Area Occupancy Mapping
Purpose: After each processing run, generate per-tiger spatial occupancy analysis including capture locations, home
ranges, centroids, and territorial overlap - visualized on a reserve map.
>>FR-3.1 Per-Tiger Capture Location Set: For every identified individual, the system must compile the
complete set of capture locations (GPS coordinates) with
associated timestamps, forming a spatiotemporal capture history.
>>FR-3.2 Home Range Estimation: The system must compute home-range estimates for each individual
using Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) at 95% (total range) and 50%
(core area) utilization distribution isopleths. Minimum Convex Polygon
(MCP) estimates must also be provided as an alternative method.
>>FR-3.3 Activity Centroid: For each individual, compute the weighted centroid of activity based on capture
frequency and recency. The centroid must be plotted on the reserve map.
>>FR-3.4 Area Estimation: The system must calculate the estimated area occupied by each individual (in sq
km) from the KDE 95% isopleth. Summary statistics (mean, median, min, max
across all individuals) must be reported.
>>FR-3.5 Territorial Overlap Analysis: The system must compute pairwise overlap indices between
individuals' home ranges using volume-of-intersection or
Bhattacharyya's affinity. Overlap must be visualized as a heatmap
matrix and as overlaid polygons on the map.
>>FR-3.6 Map Visualization: All spatial outputs must be rendered on an interactive map of the Pench Tiger
Reserve showing: reserve boundary (core and buffer), camera station
locations, per-tiger KDE polygons (color-coded by individual), activity centroids,
and overlap zones. The map must support zoom, pan, layer toggling, and
individual tiger selection.
>>FR-3.7 Export Formats: Occupancy outputs must be exportable as: GeoJSON/Shapefile (for GIS
software), PDF maps (for printed reports), CSV data tables (for spreadsheet
analysis), and KML (for Google Earth visualization). These formats are critical for
forest department staff who may use diverse tools.
>>FR-3.8 Run-by-Run Regeneration: All occupancy outputs must be regenerated from scratch on every
processing run, reflecting the latest cumulative data. Historical
snapshots of previous runs must be preserved for comparison.

6.4 Module 4: Deviation & Trend Alerting
Purpose: Compare each processing run against established individual histories and raise classified, evidence-backed
alerts for meaningful behavioural deviations.
>>FR-4.1 Historical Baseline: The system must maintain a per-individual historical profile including:
established home range, regularly visited stations, visit frequency patterns,
seasonal movement patterns, and last-seen timestamp.
>>FR-4.2 Range Shift Detection: The system must detect and alert when an individual's current home-range
centroid has shifted beyond defined thresholds: >15-20 sq km in the core
zone or >5 km linear displacement in the buffer zone. The alert must
include the magnitude and direction of the shift.
>>FR-4.3 Novel Station Alert: An alert must be raised when an individual is captured for the first time at a
camera station not previously within its established range. The alert must
indicate the distance from the individual's known range boundary.
>>FR-4.4 Buffer/Village Proximity Alert: The system must flag when an individual's captures indicate
movement into or toward buffer-zone or village-adjacent stations.
This alert is critical for human-wildlife conflict early warning.
>>FR-4.5 Prolonged Absence Alert: When a previously regular individual (defined as captured in >= 3 of the
last 5 survey cycles) is not detected in the current run, a
prolonged-absence alert must be raised. The alert must include: last
known location, days since last capture, and historical capture
frequency.
>>FR-4.6 Artefact Filtering: The alerting engine must distinguish genuine behavioural deviations from survey
artefacts, including: camera station malfunction (no images from any species),
seasonal trail inaccessibility, incomplete survey coverage, and known camera
relocation. Artefact-related non-detections must not generate false alerts.
>>FR-4.7 Alert Classification: Each alert must include: alert type (range shift / novel station / buffer
approach / prolonged absence), affected individual ID and name (if assigned),
detected change (quantified), supporting evidence (images, locations,
timestamps), confidence level (High / Medium / Low), and recommended
action.
>>FR-4.8 Alert Delivery: Alerts must be delivered via: in-dashboard notification feed, email digest to
configured recipients, SMS for high-priority alerts (buffer/village proximity), and
exportable alert reports (PDF).
>>FR-4.9 Trend Analysis: Beyond single-run deviations, the system must support multi-run trend analysis
showing: population trajectory (new individuals vs. lost individuals over time),
territory fragmentation or consolidation trends, seasonal migration patterns, and
camera network coverage effectiveness.

8. Data Architecture & Flow
8.1 Data Flow Pipeline
The system processes camera trap data through a sequential pipeline with five stages:
>>Stage 1 - Ingestion: Raw image directories are uploaded via batch upload interface or file-system mount.
EXIF metadata (timestamp, camera model) and station metadata (GPS, station ID)
are extracted and indexed.
>>Stage 2 - Blank Filtering: MegaDetector V6 processes each image. Blank images (no
animal/person/vehicle detected above threshold) are moved to quarantine.
Retained images proceed to Stage 3.
>>Stage 3 - Tiger Detection: Retained images are processed by a tiger-specific detector (fine-tuned YOLO).
Detected tigers are cropped with flank isolation. Non-tiger wildlife images are
tagged and catalogued but not processed further in this pipeline.
>>Stage 4 - Identity Matching: Flank embeddings are extracted via CNN backbone and compared against
the catalogue using Siamese network similarity scoring. Automatic matches,
ambiguous matches (human review queue), and new enrollments are
processed accordingly.
>>Stage 5 - Spatial Analytics & Alerting: Updated individual records feed into the KDE home-range engine
and deviation detection module. Maps, occupancy stats, and
alerts are generated and published to the dashboard.
8.2 Database Schema (Key Entities)
>>Individuals Table: Individual ID (PK), assigned name, first capture date, total captures count, last seen
timestamp, primary flank embedding vector, status (active/absent/unconfirmed).
>>Captures Table: Capture ID (PK), individual ID (FK), image file path, station ID (FK), GPS
latitude/longitude, capture timestamp, flank side (L/R), match confidence, review status,
reviewer ID, review timestamp.
>>Stations Table: Station ID (PK), station name, GPS coordinates, zone (core/buffer), nearest village,
deployment status, last maintenance date.
>>Alerts Table: Alert ID (PK), alert type, individual ID (FK), detected change description, evidence (JSON),
confidence level, status (new/acknowledged/resolved), assigned officer, creation timestamp.
>>Processing Runs Table: Run ID (PK), start/end timestamps, total images processed, blanks removed,
tigers identified, new individuals enrolled, alerts generated, operator ID.
8.3 Data Standards & Interoperability
>Image metadata must follow the Camtrap DP (Camera Trap Data Package) standard for interoperability
with global platforms like Wildlife Insights and LILA BC.
>Spatial data must use WGS 84 (EPSG:4326) coordinate reference system.
>All timestamps must be stored in UTC with IST conversion for display.
>Export formats must include COCO Camera Traps annotation format for ML community compatibility.

10. Literature Review & Research Foundation
The system design is grounded in extensive peer-reviewed research across computer vision, wildlife ecology, and
conservation technology. Key research areas and findings are summarized below:
10.1 Blank Image Filtering & Camera Trap AI
>MegaDetector (Microsoft AI for Earth, Beery et al., 2019): The industry-standard open-source model for
camera trap image triage. Uses YOLO-family architectures to detect animals, people, and vehicles.
Achieves >82% animal recall across diverse ecosystems. MegaDetector V6 (2024) introduced compact
variants with YOLOv9 and RT-DETR backbones for edge deployment.
>Deep learning surpasses traditional approaches: Transfer learning with ResNet-50/101 and EfficientNet
architectures achieves >95% blank classification accuracy, significantly outperforming pixel-difference and
HOG+SVM methods (Norouzzadeh et al., 2018; Tabak et al., 2019).
>Cascade architecture: Running an object detector (MegaDetector) before a species classifier dramatically
reduces computational load and false-positive rate - a two-stage pipeline is now the accepted best practice
in camera trap AI.
10.2 Individual Animal Re-Identification
>Tiger stripe uniqueness: Tiger stripe patterns are individually unique - analogous to human fingerprints -
and remain stable throughout an individual's life (Karanth & Nichols, 1998). This biological property enables
photographic capture-recapture studies.
>HotSpotter (Crall et al., 2013): A SIFT-based keypoint matching algorithm originally developed for zebra
and giraffe identification. Extracts local distinctive features ('hotspots') from body patterns. Effective but
struggles with significant pose variation and partial occlusion.
>Deep metric learning: Siamese Networks with Triplet Loss learn an embedding space where
same-individual images are close and different-individual images are distant. CNN backbones
(InceptionResNetV2, DenseNet-169) extract robust features invariant to pose, lighting, and partial occlusion
(Li et al., 2019; Schneider et al., 2020).
>TigerNet and domain-specific architectures: Specialized networks use adaptive feature enhancement to
isolate stripe topography from background noise and employ Cosine Loss for verification. These achieve
92-97% rank-1 accuracy on curated datasets (Sharma et al., 2022).
10.3 Spatial Ecology & Home Range Analysis
>Kernel Density Estimation (KDE): The standard method for utilization distribution estimation. 95% isopleths
define total home range; 50% isopleths define core areas. Autocorrelated KDE (AKDE) accounts for
temporal dependence in relocation data (Fleming et al., 2015).
>Spatially Explicit Capture-Recapture (SECR): Combines individual identification from camera traps with
spatial capture locations to jointly estimate population density and individual home-range centers (Efford,
2004; Royle et al., 2014). Implemented in R packages 'secr' and 'oSCR'.
>Camera trap occupancy modeling: Detection/non-detection data across spatial grids estimates species
presence probability while accounting for imperfect detection (MacKenzie et al., 2002). Essential for

documented and communicated to users.

degrades below defined thresholds.

13. Risk Analysis & Mitigation
>>Risk: Domain Shift in AI Models: 
-Description: MegaDetector and species classifiers may exhibit 10-60% accuracy drops when deployed in
new geographic regions with unfamiliar vegetation, lighting, and camera angles.
-Mitigation: Fine-tune models on Pench-specific training data (minimum 5,000 labeled images). Implement
continuous accuracy monitoring with automatic flagging if performance drops below thresholds.
>>Risk: Insufficient Training Data for Individual ID: 
-Description: New or rarely photographed individuals may have insufficient reference images for reliable
stripe-pattern matching.
-Mitigation: Implement a confidence-tiered matching system. Low-confidence matches are queued for
human review. Minimum 3 reference images per flank required before auto-matching is enabled for an
individual.
>>Risk: Connectivity Limitations: 
-Description: Remote camera stations have no cellular or internet connectivity, limiting real-time data
transmission.
-Mitigation: Design core processing to be fully offline-capable. Edge processing (future phase) can pre-filter
at the station. Batch uploads during periodic field visits remain the primary data flow.
>>Risk: Hardware Failure in Remote Conditions: 
-Description: Processing servers at reserve headquarters may be affected by power outages, heat,
humidity, or monsoon-related damage.
-Mitigation: Deploy UPS and surge protection. Maintain automated backups to secondary storage. Design
the system for graceful degradation and recovery.
>>Risk: Staff Adoption & Training: 
-Description: Forest department staff may resist or struggle to adopt new technology due to limited digital
literacy.
-Mitigation: Design for simplicity - key workflows must require <4 hours of training. Provide Hindi language
support. Assign dedicated on-site champions for the first 6 months.
>>Risk: False Alerts Causing Alert Fatigue: 
-Description: Excessive or inaccurate deviation alerts may cause staff to ignore genuinely critical
notifications.
-Mitigation: Implement artefact filtering to suppress false alerts from camera malfunctions or survey gaps.
Provide configurable alert thresholds and daily/weekly digest modes.
>>Risk: Data Security Breach: 
-Description: Unauthorized access to sensitive tiger location data could facilitate poaching.
-Mitigation: Encrypt all data at rest and in transit. Implement strict RBAC, audit logging, and network
isolation. Conduct periodic security audits. No public-facing endpoints.
>>Risk: Model Obsolescence: 
-Description: Rapid advances in AI may render current model architectures outdated within 2-3 years.
-Mitigation: Design modular model-serving architecture allowing hot-swap of detection and identification
models without system rebuild. Maintain abstraction layers between pipeline logic and model inference.

18. Glossary of Terms
>>AKDE: Autocorrelated Kernel Density Estimation - a home-range estimation method that accounts for
temporal autocorrelation in movement data.
>>Camtrap DP: Camera Trap Data Package - a standardized data exchange format for camera trap studies.
>>CNN: Convolutional Neural Network - a class of deep learning models particularly effective for image
recognition tasks.
>>EXIF: Exchangeable Image File Format - metadata embedded in image files containing camera settings,
timestamps, and GPS data.
>>KDE: Kernel Density Estimation - a non-parametric method for estimating the probability density function,
used here for home-range estimation.
>>LILA BC: Labeled Information Library of Alexandria: Biology and Conservation - a public repository of
labeled camera trap datasets.
>>MCP: Minimum Convex Polygon - the smallest convex polygon enclosing all capture points, used as a
simple home-range estimate.
>>MegaDetector: An open-source AI model by Microsoft AI for Earth that detects animals, people, and
vehicles in camera trap images.
>>M-STrIPES: Monitoring System for Tigers: Intensive Protection and Ecological Status - NTCA's official tiger
monitoring IT system.
>>NTCA: National Tiger Conservation Authority - the statutory body governing tiger conservation in India.
>>PostGIS: A spatial database extension for PostgreSQL enabling geographic object storage and analysis.
>>PTR: Pench Tiger Reserve.
>>RBAC: Role-Based Access Control - a security mechanism restricting system access based on user roles.
>>SECR: Spatially Explicit Capture-Recapture - a statistical framework estimating population density from
individual identification and spatial capture data.
>>Siamese Network: A neural network architecture containing two identical sub-networks used for comparing
similarity between two inputs.
>>Triplet Loss: A loss function used in metric learning that ensures an anchor is closer to a positive example
than a negative example in embedding space.
>>UD: Utilization Distribution - a probability surface representing the relative frequency of an animal's use of
space.
>>WII: Wildlife Institute of India - a research and training institution under the Ministry of Environment, Forest
and Climate Change.
>>YOLO: You Only Look Once - a family of real-time object detection neural network architectures.

3. Module 1: Blank Image Filtering Engine
3.1 Algorithm Specification
The blank filtering engine uses a two-stage approach combining object detection with statistical validation:
>>Stage A - Object Detection: Every input image is processed by MegaDetector V6 (or equivalent), which
outputs bounding boxes with class labels (animal / person / vehicle) and
confidence scores in range [0.0, 1.0].
>>Stage B - Blank Classification: An image is classified as BLANK if and only if: no bounding box from
Stage A has a confidence score >= BLANK_THRESHOLD (configurable,
default 0.2). Images with at least one detection >= threshold are classified
as SUBJECT.
>>Stage C - Boundary Review Flagging: Images with max detection confidence in the range

4. Module 2: Individual Tiger Identification Engine
4.1 Detection Pipeline
The tiger identification module uses a multi-stage detection and recognition pipeline:
>>Stage 1 - Tiger Detection: Fine-tuned YOLOv8-L model trained specifically on Indian tiger camera trap
images. Input: full-frame retained image (post-blank-filtering). Output: bounding
boxes for each tiger instance with confidence scores. Minimum detection
confidence: 0.4.
>>Stage 2 - Crop Extraction: Each detected tiger bounding box is expanded by 15% padding on all sides
(clamped to image boundaries) and cropped. The crop is resized to 448x448
pixels for downstream processing.
>>Stage 3 - Quality Assessment: Each crop is assessed for identification suitability: minimum resolution
(200x200 pre-resize), blur detection (Laplacian variance > 100), occlusion
estimation (< 30% body occluded). Low-quality crops are flagged but still
processed with reduced confidence weighting.
>>Stage 4 - Flank Detection & Isolation: A secondary model (fine-tuned segmentation network or pose
estimation) identifies the visible flank (left or right side). The flank
region is isolated as the Region of Interest (ROI) for stripe
extraction. Flank orientation (L/R) is recorded as metadata.
4.2 Stripe Pattern Feature Extraction
The stripe embedding extractor converts isolated flank images into fixed-dimensional feature vectors:
>>Architecture: Siamese Network with shared-weight CNN backbone. Primary backbone: DenseNet-169
pre-trained on ImageNet, fine-tuned on tiger re-identification dataset.
>>Alternative Backbone: InceptionResNetV2 (higher accuracy, slower inference) or EfficientNet-B4
(balanced performance). Selectable via configuration.
>>Embedding Dimension: 512-dimensional L2-normalized feature vector. Stored as float32 array in
pgvector column.
>>Training Loss: Triplet Loss with online hard-negative mining. Margin parameter: 0.3. Training requires
minimum 20 images per individual across 50+ individuals.
>>Data Augmentation: Random horizontal flip (only within same-flank sets), rotation (+/-15 degrees),
brightness/contrast jitter (+/-20%), Gaussian blur, random crop (80-100% of flank
area). Night-image-specific augmentation: gamma correction simulation.
>>Input Preprocessing: Flank crop resized to 224x224. Normalized to ImageNet mean/std. Histogram
equalization applied for night images (detected via EXIF or brightness threshold <
50).
>>Inference Speed: < 100ms per crop on GPU; < 500ms on CPU. Batched inference supported (batch size
16 on 16GB VRAM).

The matching engine compares new embeddings against the existing catalogue:
>>Similarity Metric: Cosine similarity between 512-d embedding vectors. Range: [-1.0, 1.0]. Higher = more
similar.
>>Vector Search: pgvector extension with IVFFlat index (nlist=100, nprobe=10) for approximate nearest
neighbor search. Falls back to exact search for catalogues < 500 individuals.
>>Matching Thresholds: AUTO_MATCH: cosine similarity >= 0.85 (applied automatically). REVIEW: 0.60 <=
similarity < 0.85 (queued for human review). NEW_INDIVIDUAL: similarity < 0.60
against all catalogue entries (new enrollment triggered).
>>Flank Matching Rule: Only same-flank embeddings are compared (left-to-left, right-to-right). If both flanks
are available for an individual, they are stored as separate embedding entries linked
to the same individual ID.
>>Multi-Image Consensus: When multiple crops of the same individual exist in one capture event (burst
mode), consensus matching is applied: the individual assignment is the mode of
top-1 matches across all crops, weighted by confidence.
>>Catalogue Update Policy: On confirmed match (auto or human-verified): the individual's reference
embedding is updated as a running weighted average (new embedding
contributes 10% weight). This adapts to aging-related stripe changes.
>>New Enrollment: New individuals receive auto-generated IDs: PTR-T-{NNN} (e.g., PTR-T-001). The first
capture becomes the primary reference embedding. Enrollment is marked 'provisional'
until confirmed by a second independent capture.

5. Module 3: Spatial Occupancy Analytics Engine
5.1 Home Range Computation
Home range estimation is performed for each identified individual using their complete capture history:
>>Primary Method - KDE: Fixed Kernel Density Estimation with bivariate normal kernel. Bandwidth selection:
Least Squares Cross-Validation (LSCV) or plug-in estimator. Output: Utilization
Distribution (UD) raster grid at 100m resolution.
>>Isopleths: 95% UD isopleth = total home range boundary. 50% UD isopleth = core activity area. Both
exported as GeoJSON polygons with area in sq km.
>>Secondary Method - MCP: Minimum Convex Polygon computed from all capture points. Provided as a
simpler alternative for quick visualization. Known limitation: overestimates
range, especially with outlier captures.
>>AKDE Option: Autocorrelated KDE (using ctmm R package via rpy2 bridge) available for individuals with
sufficient temporal capture data (>= 30 captures across >= 6 months). AKDE accounts for
movement autocorrelation and provides confidence intervals on area estimates.
>>Minimum Data Requirement: KDE requires >= 10 unique capture locations. Individuals with fewer
captures receive only point-map visualization (no range polygon). Warning
flag raised for estimates based on 10-20 points.
>>Centroid Computation: Weighted centroid = sum(w_i * location_i) / sum(w_i), where w_i = recency weight
(exponential decay, half-life = 90 days) * frequency weight at station. Output:
single (lat, lon) point per individual.
>>Implementation: Python scipy.stats.gaussian_kde for standard KDE. R adehabitatHR::kernelUD via rpy2
for advanced options. Shapely for polygon operations. All computation triggered after
each identification run completion.
5.2 Overlap Analysis
Territorial overlap between individuals is quantified and visualized:
>>Pairwise Overlap Index: Volume of Intersection (VI) between two individuals' UDs: VI(i,j) = integral of
min(UD_i, UD_j). Range [0, 1]. Computed for all individual pairs with overlapping
MCP bounding boxes (spatial pre-filter).
>>Bhattacharyya's Affinity (BA): Alternative overlap metric: BA = integral of sqrt(UD_i * UD_j). More
sensitive to distribution shape differences. Both VI and BA stored for each
pair.
>>Overlap Matrix: N x N matrix (N = number of active individuals) storing pairwise overlap values. Rendered
as a color-coded heatmap in the dashboard.
>>Spatial Overlap Polygons: Intersection of 95% KDE isopleths between overlapping individuals rendered
as shaded polygons on the map layer.
>>Sex-Based Filtering: If sex metadata is available, male-male, female-female, and male-female overlaps
are categorized separately for ecological interpretation.

6. Module 4: Deviation Detection & Alerting Engine
6.1 Baseline Computation
Each individual's historical baseline is computed from cumulative capture data:
>>Established Home Range: Rolling KDE 95% isopleth from all captures in the last 24 months (configurable
window). Updated after each processing run.
>>Station Visitation Profile: For each individual: list of stations where captured, visit frequency per station,
first/last visit timestamps. Stored as a JSON object in the individual's profile.
>>Expected Capture Frequency: Mean inter-capture interval (days) computed from historical data.
Individuals with >= 5 captures have a computed 'regularity score'
(coefficient of variation of inter-capture intervals).
>>Seasonal Patterns: If >= 2 years of data available: monthly capture probability estimated using
generalized linear model. Flags seasonal vs. year-round residents.
>>Baseline Freeze: Baseline is NOT updated with data from the current run until alerts have been generated.
This prevents circular baseline contamination.
6.2 Alert Rule Specifications
Four primary alert types are implemented:
Alert Type 1: Range Shift (RANGE_SHIFT)
>Trigger: Current run centroid distance from baseline centroid exceeds threshold.
>Core zone threshold: 15-20 sq km shift in KDE 95% area (configurable, default: 15 sq km).
>Buffer zone threshold: 5 km linear displacement of centroid (configurable).
>Computation: Haversine distance between current and baseline centroids. Area difference between current
and baseline KDE 95% polygons.
>Output: Shift magnitude (km), direction (bearing), area change (sq km), visualization of old vs. new range.
Alert Type 2: Novel Station (NOVEL_STATION)
>Trigger: Individual captured at a station not in their established station visitation profile.
>Computation: Set difference between current capture stations and historical station set.
>Output: Novel station ID, distance from nearest historical station, distance from baseline range boundary.
>Confidence: HIGH if novel station is > 5 km from range boundary; MEDIUM if 2-5 km; LOW if < 2 km.
Alert Type 3: Buffer/Village Proximity (BUFFER_APPROACH)
>Trigger: Individual's current captures include stations classified as 'buffer-zone' or 'village-adjacent' that are
NOT in their historical profile.
>Pre-requisite: Station metadata must include zone classification (core/buffer/village-adjacent).
>Priority: Always HIGH. Directly relevant to human-wildlife conflict early warning.
>Output: Station details, nearest village name and distance, individual's movement trajectory toward buffer.

Alert Type 4: Prolonged Absence (PROLONGED_ABSENCE)
>Trigger: Individual with regularity score >= 0.5 (captured in >= 3 of last 5 survey cycles) NOT detected in
current run.
>Grace period: If current run is the first non-detection, alert is LOW confidence. Second consecutive
non-detection: MEDIUM. Third+: HIGH.
>Output: Last known location, days since last capture, historical capture frequency, camera operational
status at last-known stations.
6.3 Artefact Filtering Logic
Before generating alerts, the following survey artefacts are checked and suppressed:
>Camera Malfunction Check: If a station produced zero images (of any species) in the current run, any
absence-based alerts referencing that station are suppressed. Alert metadata notes: 'Station offline'.
>Coverage Completeness: If the current run covers < 80% of historical stations, all
PROLONGED_ABSENCE alerts are downgraded by one confidence level. Below 50% coverage, absence
alerts are suppressed entirely.
>Known Relocations: If station metadata indicates a camera was relocated since the last run, novel-station
alerts at the new location are suppressed if the old and new locations are within 500m.
>Seasonal Adjustment: If seasonal patterns are established and the current run falls in a historically
low-capture season for an individual, absence alert confidence is reduced by one level.

9. Data Ingestion & ETL Pipeline
9.1 File Ingestion Workflow
>Ingestion supports two modes: (A) Directory scan - operator points system at a mounted filesystem path;
(B) Web upload - operator uploads ZIP archive via dashboard (max 10 GB per upload).
>Directory scan is recursive, processing all supported image files (JPEG, PNG, TIFF, BMP) in all
subdirectories.
>Each ingestion creates a new processing_run record with status 'ingesting'.
>Files are copied (not moved) from source to MinIO object storage, preserving directory structure as object
key prefixes.
>Original source files are never modified or deleted by the ingestion process.
9.2 EXIF & Metadata Extraction
>EXIF data extracted using Python Pillow (PIL) and exifread libraries.
>Extracted fields: DateTimeOriginal (capture timestamp), Make/Model (camera), ImageWidth/Height, GPS
coordinates (if embedded), Orientation.
>If EXIF timestamp is missing, file modification time is used as fallback (with 'estimated' flag).
>Station assignment: (A) If GPS is in EXIF, nearest station within 100m radius is assigned. (B) If no GPS,
station is inferred from directory name pattern (configurable regex). (C) Manual override via CSV upload
mapping filenames to stations.
>Camera serial number (if in EXIF) is recorded for camera health tracking.
9.3 Deduplication Strategy
>Perceptual hashing (pHash) computed for every ingested image (64-bit hash).
>Exact duplicates (Hamming distance = 0) are detected and skipped, with the duplicate count recorded in
the run report.
>Near-duplicates (Hamming distance <= 5) are flagged but ingested, as burst-mode frames may be
perceptually similar but contain useful pose variation.
>Cross-run deduplication: new images are checked against the last 3 runs to prevent re-processing of
previously ingested images.
9.4 Data Validation Rules
>Image must be decodable (corrupt files logged and skipped).
>Minimum resolution: 640x480 pixels. Smaller images logged as 'low_resolution' and processed with quality
flag.
>Maximum file size: 50 MB per image (larger files logged and skipped - likely non-camera-trap).
>Timestamp sanity: capture time must be within [2010-01-01, current_date + 1 day]. Out-of-range
timestamps flagged.
>GPS sanity: if GPS is present, coordinates must fall within Pench bounding box (+/- 0.5 degrees tolerance).

11. Security Architecture
11.1 Encryption Standards
>Data at rest: AES-256-GCM encryption for PostgreSQL (Transparent Data Encryption) and MinIO
(server-side encryption with KMS). Encryption keys stored in a dedicated key file with 0600 permissions.
>Data in transit: TLS 1.3 for all HTTP traffic (Nginx termination). TLS 1.2+ for internal PostgreSQL
connections (sslmode=verify-full).
>Backup encryption: All database dumps and image backups encrypted with GPG (RSA-4096) before writing
to external storage.
>Sensitive fields: GPS coordinates in API responses are rounded to 3 decimal places (~111m precision) for
RANGE_OFFICER and below roles. Full precision available only to ADMIN and BIOLOGIST roles.

>ADMIN: Full CRUD on all resources. User management. System configuration. Purge operations. Raw
GPS export.

access.
>RANGE_OFFICER: Read all. Write alerts (acknowledge/resolve). Export reports (GPS rounded). Upload
images.
>FIELD_STAFF: Upload images. View dashboard (no GPS). View assigned alerts.
>VIEWER: Read-only dashboard and reports. No GPS. No export.
>All role assignments and changes are logged in the audit trail with actor ID and timestamp.

12. Performance Engineering
12.1 Benchmarks & SLAs
Performance targets for each system component:
>>Blank Filtering: Throughput: >= 1,000 images/minute (GPU), >= 100 images/minute (CPU). Latency p95:
< 60ms/image (GPU).
>>Tiger Detection: Throughput: >= 500 images/minute (GPU). Latency p95: < 120ms/image.
>>Embedding Extraction: Throughput: >= 200 crops/minute (GPU). Latency p95: < 300ms/crop.
>>Vector Search: Latency p95: < 50ms for top-10 nearest neighbor query against 10,000 embeddings.
>>KDE Computation: < 30 seconds per individual with 100 capture points. < 30 minutes for full reserve (80
individuals).
>>API Response: p95 latency < 200ms for read endpoints. < 500ms for write endpoints. < 2s for complex
spatial queries.
>>Dashboard Load: Initial page load < 3 seconds. Map render with all layers < 5 seconds.
12.2 GPU Optimization
>Mixed-precision inference (FP16) enabled for all detection and embedding models. Reduces VRAM usage
by ~40% with < 1% accuracy impact.
>Dynamic batching: images are accumulated in a queue and dispatched to GPU in optimal batch sizes
(determined by available VRAM at runtime).
>Model warmup: on service startup, each model processes 5 dummy images to initialize CUDA kernels and
memory allocators.
>ONNX Runtime with TensorRT execution provider as optional high-performance inference backend for
NVIDIA GPUs.
>GPU memory monitoring: if VRAM usage exceeds 90%, batch size is automatically reduced. OOM errors
trigger graceful fallback to smaller batch size.
12.3 Caching Strategy
>Redis cache for: API response caching (TTL: 5 minutes for dashboard data, 1 hour for catalogue), session
tokens, rate limit counters.
>Thumbnail cache: 256x256 WebP thumbnails generated on first access and cached in MinIO. Subsequent
requests served directly.
>Spatial query cache: KDE polygons (GeoJSON) cached per individual with invalidation on new data
ingestion.
>Embedding cache: frequently queried reference embeddings loaded into Redis (serialized numpy arrays)
for sub-millisecond retrieval.
12.4 Batch Processing Optimization
>Pipeline parallelism: Stage 1 (blank filtering) and Stage 2 (tiger detection) run on separate Celery queues.

15. Disaster Recovery & Backup
15.1 Backup Strategy
>>Database: Automated pg_dump every 24 hours (2 AM IST). Full dump + WAL archiving for point-in-time
recovery. Backup compressed with zstd and encrypted with GPG.
>>Object Storage (Images): MinIO replication to external NAS/USB drive. Incremental sync using mc mirror
(MinIO Client). Run weekly or after each major processing run.
>>Configuration: Docker Compose files, environment variables, model weights, and SSL certificates backed
up to version-controlled repository (Git). Updated on every configuration change.
>>Retention: Daily database backups: 30 days. Weekly image backups: 90 days. Monthly full system
backup: 1 year.
15.2 Recovery Procedures
>>RPO (Recovery Point Objective): Database: 24 hours (last backup). Images: 7 days (last sync).
Configuration: 0 hours (version controlled).
>>RTO (Recovery Time Objective): Full system restoration from backup: < 4 hours on same hardware. < 8
hours on replacement hardware (including OS and Docker setup).
>>Procedure: (1) Restore Docker Compose configuration from Git. (2) Restore PostgreSQL from latest
dump. (3) Restore MinIO data from external storage. (4) Verify data integrity with checksums.
(5) Run health checks on all services. (6) Resume processing from last checkpoint.
15.3 Data Integrity
>All database backups verified with pg_restore --list (structure check) and row-count comparison against live
database.
>MinIO objects verified with MD5 checksums on backup media vs. live storage (sampled 1% per run).
>Monthly disaster recovery drill: restore from backup on isolated VM and run integration test suite.

maintenance alert.
18. Technical Constraints & Limitations
18.1 Known Limitations
>Night image quality: infrared camera trap images have lower contrast and no color information, reducing
both detection and re-identification accuracy by an estimated 10-20%.
>Single-flank matching: the system can only match flanks of the same side (L-to-L, R-to-R). A tiger
photographed only from the left cannot be matched to a catalogue entry with only right-flank images.
Cross-flank matching is a research frontier not yet production-ready.
>Cub identification: stripe patterns in cubs may change subtly during growth (first 12-18 months).

Re-identification accuracy for cubs is expected to be lower. Cubs should be tracked with 'provisional' status
until patterns stabilize.
>Occlusion and pose: heavily occluded tigers (>50% body hidden by vegetation) or tigers in unusual poses
(swimming, climbing) may fail detection or produce low-quality crops unsuitable for identification.
>Camera trap density: home range estimates are only as accurate as the spatial distribution of camera

18.2 Technical Constraints
>Single GPU deployment: the system is designed for single-GPU inference. Multi-GPU scaling requires

>PostgreSQL scaling: single-node PostgreSQL is adequate for the expected data volume (< 1M captures).

>Real-time processing: the system operates in batch mode (process after data collection). Near-real-time
processing (< 1 hour latency) requires edge computing infrastructure (Phase 2).
>Internet dependency: alert delivery (email, SMS) requires internet connectivity. On-premise dashboard
functions fully offline, but remote access requires VPN or internet.
18.3 Scalability Boundaries
>Tested for: up to 500,000 images per processing run, 200 individual tiger profiles, 10,000+ embeddings,
500 camera stations.
>Beyond these thresholds: database query optimization, embedding index tuning, and potentially horizontal
scaling will be required.
>Multi-reserve deployment: each reserve should have its own database instance. Cross-reserve queries via
federated query layer (future phase).

1. Design Philosophy & Visual Identity
1.1 Core Principles
The design system for the Tiger Movement Intelligence System is built on five core principles that balance technical
precision with the organic gravitas of wildlife conservation:
>>Precision: Every interaction, shadow, and animation is deliberately crafted. The system communicates
'high-performance conservation technology' - fast, reliable, and obsessively detailed. No
element is arbitrary.
>>Atmospheric Depth: The interface exists in layered three-dimensional space. Surfaces float above
ambient backgrounds, illuminated by soft gradient light pools inspired by the forest
canopy at dusk. This creates visual hierarchy without harsh separators.
>>Data Sovereignty: Tiger data is sacred. The design elevates data - maps, images, statistics - as the
primary visual content. Chrome and decoration are minimized. The interface is a clear
lens onto the data, never competing with it.
>>Organic Warmth: Unlike cold corporate dashboards, this system uses warm amber/gold accents
(#B8822E) and deep forest tones to create an emotional connection to the conservation
mission. The palette evokes twilight in a tiger reserve.
>>Operational Clarity: Forest officers and biologists need immediate comprehension. Alert states are
unmistakable. Status is glanceable. Critical actions are prominent. The design
prioritizes function, then elevates it with craft.
1.2 The "Deep Wilderness" Aesthetic
The visual identity is named 'Deep Wilderness' - a fusion of the Linear/Modern dark interface aesthetic with the
atmosphere of a tiger reserve at nightfall. Imagine the control room of a high-tech conservation operation: deep
near-black surfaces (#060607) lit by amber-gold status glows and the soft blue light of satellite maps.
>Dark, but not oppressive: Near-black backgrounds with subtle warmth from forest green and amber
undertones. Never pure black (#000000).
>Technical, but not sterile: Monospace data readouts sit alongside warm typography. Grid overlays suggest
precision; gradient blobs suggest organic life.
>Urgent, but not anxious: Alert states use carefully calibrated orange/red tones that command attention
without inducing panic. Amber is the dominant accent - the color of a tiger's eye.
>Cinematic, but functional: Multi-layer backgrounds and floating elements create atmosphere, but every pixel
earns its place through functional purpose.
1.3 Signature Visual Elements
These elements MUST be present for visual authenticity:
>>Ambient Forest Blobs: Large, heavily blurred gradient shapes (900-1400px) float slowly across
backgrounds, simulating light filtering through a forest canopy. Colors: amber at
12-20% opacity, forest green at 8-15%, deep indigo at 10%.
>>Tiger-Eye Accent Glow: Interactive elements (buttons, selected cards, active tabs) emit a soft amber glow

- reminiscent of a tiger's eye catching light in darkness. This is the signature

>>Multi-Layer Card Shadows: Cards use 3-4 shadow layers: 1px border highlight + soft diffuse shadow +
ambient darkness + optional amber accent glow on hover. Never single-layer

>>Map-Centric Layout: The map is always the largest visual element on spatial pages. It is not confined to a

tiny (4-8px max). Scale changes: 0.98-1.02. Nothing bounces.
1.4 Anti-Patterns (What to Avoid)
>Pure black (#000000) backgrounds - always use near-blacks like #060607 or #030304.
>Pure white (#FFFFFF) text - use off-white #EDEDEF to reduce harshness against dark backgrounds.
>Single-layer shadows - always combine border highlight + diffuse shadow + ambient glow.
>Flat backgrounds - always layer gradients, noise texture, and ambient blobs.
>Large hover movements (>8px) - this is precise, not playful.
>Uniform grids - use varied card sizes in bento layouts for visual rhythm.
>Overuse of accent color - amber/gold is for highlights and interaction, not decoration. Most UI is
monochromatic.
>Bouncy animations - use expo-out easing, not spring physics.
>Generic chart colors - data visualization uses the curated tiger identity palette (Section 2.4).
>Cramped mobile layouts - generous padding and touch-friendly tap targets (44px minimum) on mobile.

>Contrast ratios: primary text (#EDEDEF on #060607) = ~15:1. Secondary text (#9CA3AF on #060607) =

>Focus indicators: visible focus ring on all interactive elements. Ring: 2px solid accent, 2px offset matching
background color. Never hidden.
>Keyboard navigation: all interactive elements reachable via Tab. Logical tab order. Escape closes
modals/dropdowns. Arrow keys navigate within components.
>Screen readers: all images have alt text. Icons have aria-label. Status changes announced via aria-live
regions. Tables have proper th/scope.
>Color independence: never rely solely on color. Always pair with icons, labels, or patterns. Alert types
identified by icon + text + color.
>Reduced motion: @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) disables all animations and transitions.

4. Authentication Flow
Complete authentication flow from first visit to authenticated session:
[ VISIT / ]
User navigates to TigerWatch. Next.js middleware checks for valid JWT in cookies.
   |
   v  No valid JWT found
[ REDIRECT /login ]
User sees login page. Dark themed with TigerWatch logo, ambient background blobs, centered login card.
   |
   v  User enters credentials
[ POST /api/v1/auth/login ]
Frontend sends { username, password } to API. Shows loading spinner on button.
   |
   v  API returns { access_token, refresh_token, user }
[ STORE TOKENS ]
access_token stored in httpOnly cookie (1hr expiry). refresh_token in httpOnly cookie (7d). User object stored
in React context.
   |
   v  Redirect to /dashboard
[ DASHBOARD LOADS ]
Shell layout renders. Sidebar, TopBar, and Dashboard content load. Initial data fetched.
Token Refresh Flow
>When API returns 401: frontend interceptor calls POST /api/v1/auth/refresh with refresh_token.
>If refresh succeeds: new access_token set, original request retried automatically.
>If refresh fails (expired/invalid): user redirected to /login with message 'Session expired. Please log in
again.'
>Auto-refresh: proactive refresh when access_token has < 5 minutes remaining (checked on each API call).
Logout Flow
>User clicks Logout in sidebar bottom area.
>POST /api/v1/auth/logout called. Tokens cleared from cookies. React context reset.
>Redirect to /login with success toast 'Logged out successfully'.
Role-Based UI Gating
>ADMIN: sees all nav items including Settings, User Management. Can start runs, purge data, manage
models.
>BIOLOGIST: sees all except User Management. Can approve reviews, manage catalogue, configure alert
rules.
>RANGE_OFFICER: sees Dashboard, Catalogue (read-only), Map, Alerts (can acknowledge/resolve),
Stations (read-only).

7. State Management & Data Flow
7.1 Client-Side State Architecture
State management uses React Context + useReducer for global state, with SWR (or TanStack Query) for server state
caching:
>>AuthContext: User object, JWT tokens, role, login/logout actions. Persisted in httpOnly cookies. Checked
by middleware on every route change.
>>UIContext: Sidebar collapsed state, active theme, notification preferences, mobile menu open state.
Persisted in localStorage.
>>Server State (SWR): All API data fetched and cached by SWR with stale-while-revalidate strategy. Cache
keys match API URLs. Automatic revalidation on window focus. Manual revalidation
triggered after mutations (e.g., after confirming a review match, revalidate catalogue
and review queue).
7.2 Real-Time Data (WebSocket)
>WebSocket connection established on authenticated shell mount. Single connection, multiplexed channels.
>Channels: (1) processing-progress: live run stats updates. (2) new-alerts: instant alert notifications. (3)
system-health: GPU/memory/disk status.
>On new alert message: TopBar bell badge increments + toast notification + if user is on /alerts page, new
card inserts at top with animation.
>On processing progress: if user is on /processing/runs/[id], live counters and progress bar update. If on
/dashboard, processing indicator in TopBar pulses.
>Connection lost: subtle amber banner 'Reconnecting...' + automatic retry with exponential backoff (1s, 2s,
4s, 8s, max 30s).
7.3 Data Mutation Flow
Standard pattern for all write operations:
>1. User action triggers mutation (button click, form submit, drag-drop).
>2. Optimistic UI update (where safe): UI reflects expected state immediately.
>3. API call fires (POST/PUT/DELETE).
>4. On success: SWR cache invalidated for affected resources. Toast: 'Action completed'.
>5. On failure: optimistic update rolled back. Error toast with message. If 401, trigger token refresh flow.

10. Error States & Empty States
10.1 Error Handling Patterns
>>API Error (400/422): Toast notification: red, with error message from API response. Form fields: highlight
errored fields with red border + inline error message below field.
>>Unauthorized (401): Silent token refresh attempt. If refresh fails: redirect to /login with message 'Session
expired'.
>>Forbidden (403): Toast: 'You do not have permission for this action.' No redirect - user stays on current
page.
>>Not Found (404): Full-page 404 component: 'Page not found' illustration + 'The page you're looking for
doesn't exist.' + 'Back to Dashboard' button.
>>Server Error (500): Full-page error component: 'Something went wrong' illustration + 'Our system
encountered an error. Please try again.' + 'Retry' button + 'Contact Support' link.
>>Network Error: Banner at top: amber, 'Connection lost. Retrying...' with subtle pulse animation. Auto-retry
with exponential backoff. Banner disappears when connection restored.
>>Timeout: Toast: 'Request timed out. Please try again.' + 'Retry' button in toast.
10.2 Empty State Designs
Every list/grid/table page must have a designed empty state:
>>Dashboard (first use): Welcome illustration + 'Welcome to TigerWatch' + 'Start by uploading your first
batch of camera trap images.' + 'Upload Images' primary button.
>>Catalogue (no tigers): Tiger silhouette illustration + 'No tigers identified yet' + 'Process camera trap
images to start building your catalogue.' + 'Start Processing' button.
>>Catalogue (filtered, no results): Filter illustration + 'No tigers match your filters' + 'Try adjusting your
search criteria.' + 'Clear Filters' button.
>>Alert Feed (no alerts): Shield-check illustration + 'All clear!' + 'No alerts at this time. System is monitoring
for deviations.' + muted text: 'Alerts are generated after each processing run.'
>>Review Queue (empty): Celebration illustration + 'All caught up!' + 'No images pending review. Great
work!' + 'Back to Dashboard' button.
>>Run History (no runs): Processor illustration + 'No processing runs yet' + 'Upload camera trap images to
start your first run.' + 'Upload Images' button.
>>Stations (no stations): Map pin illustration + 'No stations configured' + 'Import your camera trap station
locations to get started.' + 'Import Stations' button.
